Verdict — go, but go narrow

Attend the Summit (Sept 16, $250, one person). Treat the Festival (Sept 17) as an optional add-on, not a reason to go.

1. Read the room honestly: this is a non-alcoholic event, and BevGenie sells to beverage alcohol. Most of the 80-person Summit and the 44-brand Festival floor are single-product NA startups, sommeliers, retailers and a dietitian — off-ICP. If we go expecting a lead-gen bonanza, we'll be disappointed. This is a market-education and relationship-seeding play, on the single trend (moderation / NA / functional) that our own investor narrative leans on.

2. But the few right people justify the $250. Three verified reasons to be in that room: (a) Brian Rosen / InvestBev — the one genuine alcohol capital allocator present, a plausible BevGenie investor and a source of portfolio-company customers; (b) Bill Shufelt (Athletic Brewing) and Laura Taylor (Mingle Mocktails) — the two NA founders who actually run through alcohol three-tier distributors and national chains, i.e. real depletion/category-review problems; (c) the moderation thesis itself, straight from the founders driving it.

3. The sharpest single hook is already in hand. Mingle's national distributor is RNDC — the wholesaler now being dismantled (sold 11 markets to Reyes, exiting Illinois; see Competitive Landscape · Jul 2026). A brand whose distribution is mid-disruption is a live, unscripted demonstration of exactly what BevGenie surfaces. That's a conversation opener, not a pitch.

4. The non-obvious Festival value isn't the NA startups — it's their parents. Several sponsor brands are NA lines of real three-tier alcohol companies: Asahi Group, Trinchero (FRE + Luminara + Seaglass — three brands), Cantine Riunite (Riunite + Maschio), and Lucas Bols (Fluere). Those parents are ICP. Worth walking the Trade hour (5–6pm) to see who staffs those booths — but temper expectations: a brand ambassador at a tasting table is not a buyer.

🧭 The honest strategic question: why would a BevAlc company go to a non-alc event?

Worth answering plainly, because the naïve answer ("these are all potential customers!") is wrong and would waste the day.

  • Not because NA startups are customers. A DTC mocktail brand in a few thousand doors has no depletion base worth analyzing. Most of this room is too small for decision intelligence to matter.
  • Because the distribution rails are shared. The brands that matter (Athletic, Mingle) run through the same alcohol distributors (RNDC, national wholesalers) and the same chain retailers as spirits and beer. For those brands, the category-review and distributor-QBR half of BevGenie's value prop applies directly — even though the TTB-excise / NABCA-control-state half does not, since NA sits outside that data.
  • Because the moderation trend is our own tailwind. Our investor narrative rests on the mix fragmenting toward NA/RTD/functional (verified: IWSR, Jul 14 — participation flat, intensity structurally declining). This is the room where that trend's operators and capital gather. Being fluent in it strengthens the pitch; being absent from it is a small tell.
  • Because the big alcohol parents are here. Asahi, Trinchero, Riunite and Bols showing up in NA is the clearest evidence that ICP-scale companies treat this as a real category — and those parents, not their NA SKUs, are the customers.

So the trip is defensible — as intelligence and relationships, priced at $250. It is not a pipeline event. Set expectations accordingly.
