🗓 Meeting plan — sequence + content for first 3 conversations

Meeting 1 · listening session
"Tell me what you're seeing from inside Diageo NA"

Goal: Learn + signal that BevGenie respects his expertise. Not: pitch or demo.

Opening (60 sec): "Stephen — your 'Seven Forces' piece + 'Alcohol Is Not Broken, It Is Mispriced' both land directly on what we're building. We're early-stage; we have one validated customer (Sera Luce) and we're building the agentic intelligence layer for BevAlc supplier commercial teams. Before I tell you anything more, I'd love your read on what an SVP of Marketing & Innovation at Diageo would actually find useful — and what's noise."

Questions to bring:

  • "Inside Diageo NA, what's the daily-friction problem in commercial workflows that nobody's solved yet?"
  • "Which 'reshaping forces' from your article would BevGenie's customers feel first?"
  • "Of the tools you've evaluated for the commercial side, what consistently disappoints?"
  • "If a startup walked in with one specific BevAlc-agentic-AI workflow, which one would have a chance of getting attention internally — and which would be DOA?"
  • "Outside Diageo — who are 1-2 people in the industry whose perspective you'd want me to hear?"

Close (60 sec): "This was incredibly useful. Can I send you a one-pager that takes your framing and shows how we'd apply it? No pressure — just want you to see the actual product mapped to what you said."

Meeting 2 · demo + reciprocal value
"Here's how your framing changed our roadmap"

Goal: Show BevGenie executes on input. Earn the right to ask for the advisor relationship.

Structure: 30-min product walkthrough where Shyam explicitly references Stephen's points from Meeting 1. "When you said X, that made us re-think Y — here's what changed." Then 15 min open feedback. Then propose formal advisor relationship as the close.

What to demo: the single sharpest workflow — likely the distributor-brief-generation agent or the competitive-intelligence agent. Not the full platform tour. One thing, done well, with clear before/after.

Meeting 3 · the pilot conversation
"What would it take to get one Diageo NA team using this for 6 weeks?"

Goal: Convert relationship into a concrete pilot path.

Frame: "Stephen — given what you've seen, we'd love to put this in front of one of your NA commercial teams for 6 weeks at no cost. We want the feedback; you get the option of a real tool if it works. What would need to be true for that conversation to be possible?" Listen to the answer carefully — it tells you exactly what Diageo's procurement / legal / IT / commercial-leadership chain looks like for new-tool evaluation.

⚠️ Red flags + how to handle

  • Sitting Diageo SVP = employment constraints. He cannot formally advise startups Diageo could acquire or compete with without internal approval. Surface this in Meeting 2: "Are there any internal-approval steps you'd want to run before we formalize?" Respect the answer.
  • Marketing & Innovation org ≠ Commercial / Sales org. The Diageo NA budget for new commercial-platform software likely sits with the President of US Spirits (Stephen Rust, separate Stephen) or his org, not with Marketing & Innovation. Stephen White can be an internal champion but is not the ultimate budget holder. Plan the pilot path accordingly — he gets us in the door; conversion still needs the commercial-side decision-maker.
  • 22-year corporate insider blind spots. He may underestimate the urgency / risk-tolerance of pre-seed startup operations. Don't take his "Diageo would need 6 months for that" timeline as a binding constraint on BevGenie's own timeline.
  • Innovation roles sometimes mean "evaluate but don't deploy." If after Meeting 3 there's no concrete pilot path, downgrade expectations: he's a valuable advisor + intro source, not a customer path. Don't anchor the GTM plan on him.
  • Confidentiality: Diageo internals are sensitive. Don't ask him for proprietary numbers, internal vendor RFP details, or competitor-bid information. Keep the conversation at the "category insight" level, not "Diageo's actual data" level. He'll trust you more for showing this discipline.
  • The "I'll think about it" risk. Senior corporate executives are polite. If he says "let me consider it" after Meeting 2, follow up in 7 days with a concrete next step proposal — don't let it drift into 6 months of polite ambiguity.
