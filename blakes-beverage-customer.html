2. The opening — their own market banding this is the message

Blake’s publishes its 45 states in three volume bands, footnoted “VIP depletions, trailing 12 months, August 2026”. This is the single most useful artefact in the whole research pass — it tells you exactly how they see their own business, in their own words, derived from their own data.

BandCountStates
Core markets10TX, MI, NC, NY, OH, AR, TN, FL, PA, IL
Growing12GA, IN, OR, SC, VA, WI, MN, MO, MD, AZ, NJ, KY
In distribution23OK, SD, NM, AL, DC, KS, NV, CA, WA, CO, NH, IA, NE, MT, ND, MA, LA, CT, WV, ID, RI, VT, AK
Why this is the opening and not a footnote. A company that bands its markets off trailing-twelve-month depletions is not a company you teach about data. It is a company that has already done the hard part and is now sitting on the harder question: twenty-three states where the product is merely present, twelve where it is moving, and a finite amount of sales attention to allocate between them. VIP tells them what happened. It does not tell them which of the twelve to push, which of the twenty-three to quietly exit, or which account in Georgia looks like the accounts that worked in Tennessee. That gap is the entire product.

3. Why he is an unusually good fit five reasons, in order of force

  • He is building the same argument in another industry. Aloan is an AI-native commercial underwriting platform — financial spreading, ratio calculation, policy compliance, credit memo generation, covenant monitoring. Its founding story is that “a lot of lending still happens by hand.” Swap “lending” for “beverage commercial teams” and that is our deck. He has presented at FinovateSpring 2026 and FinovateFall 2026. [aloan.ai, Finovate]
  • Three brands, four regions, one P&L. His own strategy quote: “We have these geographical brands that can really speak to the local trends and flavor profiles of the Pac-Northwest or Austin, Texas or the Midwest or the East Coast.” The plan is to spot a local winner and scale it nationally with company resources. “Which local signal is real enough to scale?” is a depletion-data question, and getting it wrong is expensive. [Brewbound Mar 18 2024]
  • A brand-new route to market where they have least visibility. Giddy Up launched Mar 11 2026 under Austin Eastciders: an 11% ABV wine-based punch, 19.2 oz single-serve, $3.49, aimed at convenience stores, pushed through SXSW and Willie Nelson’s Luck Ranch. Single-serve c-store is a completely different execution problem from 4-packs in grocery — more accounts, faster velocity, far thinner visibility on who actually reorders. [Bev Info Group, Business Wire, Mar 2026]
  • They have form in fourth-category RTD. Before Giddy Up there was Tuesday Cocktail Club (Avid, Feb 2024, 10.5% ABV wine-based RTD, Oregon and Washington). Avid co-founder Samantha Roberts: “We were missing this whole segment of the RTD category that was going through the roof.” [Brewbound Mar 18 2024]
  • The size is right. ~$60M and ~100 employees is big enough to feel portfolio-level pain across 45 states, and small enough that there is no internal data team to build this. And they are “actively looking” for strategic M&A — every acquisition makes the integration problem worse. [Brewbound Mar 20 2026]

5. The category context so the framing doesn’t insult him

  • Cider is small and roughly flat. About $502M in total US multi-outlet sales for 2025 (Circana), recovering modestly after a -1.8% dollar decline in 2024, with off-premise dollars +1.9% and volume +1.7% late in 2025. Cider holds roughly 1.12% share of category dollars, and Circana flags low innovation as the drag.
  • The neighbours are worse. FMBs -3.6%; prepared cocktail case volume -5.2% and FMB/seltzer volume -10.9% (NIQ, Aug 2026); craft beer case volume -4.3% in early Oct 2026.
  • What that means for the message. Blake’s is the “fastest growing” independent cidery in the US in a category with about one percent share and no tailwind. Growth in that position comes from execution, not from the category lifting you. That is the honest, flattering and accurate frame — and it is why the twelve “Growing” states matter so much. Never open with “cider is declining.” It isn’t, it is his life’s work, and he is beating it. [Circana via Bev Industry, NIQ, Vinetur]

6. The follow-up draft, and the reasoning behind it

The Aug 27 note led with “free trial in exchange for feedback.” It got no reply. The diagnosis is not that the offer was wrong — it is that it asked a CEO to evaluate a tool, which is work. A follow-up has one job: say something about his business he will recognise as true, and make the ask smaller than the first one.

Draft — short version, recommended Hi Andrew — following up on my note from August, with something more specific than a trial offer.

Your About page bands all 45 states into core, growing and in-distribution off trailing-twelve VIP depletions. That is a sharper view of your own footprint than most companies your size have, so I’ll skip the part where I tell you that you need data.

The part we work on is the next question: of the twelve states in “growing”, which ones actually look like Tennessee did before it became core — and which of the twenty-three are just shipping. Across three brands and four regions that answer lives in a lot of places at once, which I think is close to what you meant in March about still needing to strengthen cohesion across the portfolios.

Given what you’re building at Aloan you don’t need the AI argument from me. Worth 20 minutes to compare notes? Happy to come with a read on your growing markets rather than a demo.
Why each line is there. The About-page open proves we did the work and flatters a real strength — it cannot read as a template. “I’ll skip the part where I tell you that you need data” disarms the thing every vendor says to him. Naming Tennessee — a core state — against the growing twelve makes the problem concrete in his own geography. The March quote shows we read his trade coverage, and it lets him agree with himself rather than with us. The Aloan line is the close: it signals we know who he is, and it removes the entire “does AI really work” conversation in one sentence. “A read on your growing markets rather than a demo” inverts the original ask — we bring work to him instead of asking him to do some.
⚠ Three things not to do Do not mention that he followed the BevGenie page. It is a real signal and the reason to send now, but saying it out loud reads as surveillance and kills the tone.
Do not name Mitchell Barnard until the Aloan link is confirmed. If it is the same person it is a strong second thread; if it is not, it is an embarrassing error in a first real conversation.
Do not lead with the free trial again. It is still the eventual offer, but it was the ask that got no reply, and repeating it adds nothing new to the thread.

7. What we could not verify stated plainly

  • Whether Andrew is formally a co-founder of Aloan. His LinkedIn says Co-Founder, Sept 2025–present, and a PR Web release names him among the founders — but Aloan’s own team page does not list him, and its AI-info page names only Mitch Barnard, Tim Diamond and Gerrit Yntema. Safe to reference “what you’re building at Aloan”, because that is his own public claim. Not safe to call him a founder in writing.
  • Whether Mitchell Barnard (President, Blake’s) is Mitch Barnard (CEO, Aloan). Same name, same network, overlapping dates, and Marty Blake — who co-founded Crestmark Bank and helped grow it to a $316M sale in 2018 — sits on Aloan’s board as Lead Independent Director, which makes a family-adjacent network very likely. Still an inference. Confirm before using.
  • Nothing public about their current analytics stack beyond VIP. We know they use VIP depletions because they footnote it. We do not know what sits on top of it, whether anyone owns it internally, or whether they have looked at alternatives.
  • No 2026 revenue or case figure. The $54M/$60M numbers are 2024-vintage projections; the ~2M cases and ~100 employees come from their About page and are undated beyond “August 2026” for distribution.
  • Why the August message got no reply. Unknowable. He may not have seen it. He followed the BevGenie page after it, which is the only evidence we have and it points the right way.
  • They were not at NBWA. Checked the full 172-exhibitor list from the NBWA brief — no Blake’s, Austin Eastciders, Avid or Giddy Up. No shared-event hook to use.
